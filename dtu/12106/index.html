WEEK 04 / CLASS 25 SEPTEMBER

Carbon footprint & environmental indicators

Calculate a footprint and understand what drives the result.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. An inventory becomes an impact through factors

    Multiply activity quantities by compatible impact factors and sum over the boundary.

  2. The comparison includes more than operation

    The mobility exercise compares production and use, illustrating why a cleaner operating phase can coexist with production burdens.

  3. Carbon is one indicator

    A climate result does not cover every environmental effect.

WEEK 06 / CLASS 9 OCTOBER

Economic dimensions

Compare lifetime costs, test assumptions, and understand decision weights.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Purchase price is not life-cycle cost

    Life Cycle Costing includes costs over the chosen lifetime and boundary.

  2. Sensitivity reveals decision drivers

    Changing electricity prices or lifetime can change which option is cheapest.

  3. MCDA makes trade-offs explicit

    Multi-Criteria Decision Assessment and AHP combine criteria using preferences or weights.

WEEK 07 / CLASS 23 OCTOBER

Planetary boundaries & absolute sustainability

Ask whether an option is sustainable, as well as whether it is better.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Better does not necessarily mean sustainable

    Relative assessment asks which option has lower impact.

  2. Allocation is a consequential choice

    Translating a planetary boundary to a product or activity requires a sharing principle.

  3. Compare like units and scales

    A footprint and its allocated boundary must describe compatible quantities, time periods and indicators.

Practise

A short distinction between “lower impact” and “within the allocated boundary”.

WEEK 08 / CLASS 30 OCTOBER

Resource management, scarcity & criticality

Understand the resource risks behind your system.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Renewable resources can still be overused

    Sustainable extraction depends on regeneration, not merely on whether a resource is labelled renewable.

  2. Extraction has wider impacts

    A sustainable yield does not guarantee a low biodiversity or environmental footprint.

  3. Criticality is broader than physical scarcity

    The criticality reading considers resource risks and vulnerability.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

Suggested · 30–60 min

Screen three resources in your project

List three relevant inputs such as electricity, water or a process material. For each, identify a supply or regeneration concern, a wider environmental concern and the quality of your evidence.

YOUR OUTPUT

A three-row resource-risk screen that can inform the case discussion.

WEEK 10 / CLASS 13 NOVEMBER

Energy systems & decarbonisation

See how electricity supply and demand change your climate result.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Electricity impact depends on the mix

    Weight each technology’s life-cycle emissions by its share of production.

  2. Low operating emissions do not mean zero life-cycle impact

    Electricity technologies have construction and supply-chain impacts too.

  3. Intensity and total emissions are different

    A lower-carbon mix can still produce substantial emissions when demand is high.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

Suggested · 30–60 min

Change one electricity-mix scenario

Open Planetary CO2 boundary – Electricity Technology Mix. Inspect the baseline and test one change in technology shares while keeping the shares consistent; compare intensity and total emissions.

YOUR OUTPUT

Two scenarios and a sentence on the role of demand versus technology mix.

WEEK 11 / CLASS 20 NOVEMBER

Circular economy

Test whether a circular strategy actually improves sustainability.

Full weekly plan ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Circularity is a strategy, not an impact result

    Extending use, recovery or recycling can change resource flows.

  2. Specify what changes in the system

    Identify the material, process or service affected, the recovery rate and any extra energy or processing.

  3. Readiness affects implementation

    The module introduces ready2LOOP and the Circularity Strategy Scanner.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

Suggested · 30–60 min

Screen one circular strategy

Choose one relevant option for your actual microfabrication project: for example longer use, recovery of a process material or reuse. State the necessary assumptions, a potential benefit and an added burden.

YOUR OUTPUT

A short strategy proposal with a condition under which it would fail to improve sustainability.

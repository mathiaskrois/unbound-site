01 / UNDERSTAND

What matters this week

Essential takeaways, synthesised from the module and case instructions.

01

An inventory becomes an impact through factors

Multiply activity quantities by compatible impact factors and sum over the boundary. Check units carefully: kWh × kg CO₂e/kWh gives kg CO₂e, whereas a factor in grams needs conversion.

02

The comparison includes more than operation

The mobility exercise compares production and use, illustrating why a cleaner operating phase can coexist with production burdens. Apply the same boundary discipline to chip manufacture and chip use.

03

Carbon is one indicator

A climate result does not cover every environmental effect. Use carbon to find important contributions, while keeping other relevant impacts visible in the case assessment.

04

Use the current spreadsheet

Some process numbers differ from the videos. Match the activity name to the process in the current workbook, and use the supplied solutions to check your work.

FOR YOUR MICROFABRICATION PROJECT

Finalise the Task 1 scope before the 30 September deadline. The carbon exercise and self-assessment are explicitly practice, not assessed results.

02 / PRACTISE

Complete one mobility comparison

30–60 min · Suggested

Open the carbon-footprint mobility workbook and Tool instructions 1–3. Fill the highlighted inputs for the gasoline/electric comparison, inspect the largest contribution and compare against the solution.

FINISH WITH

One checked comparison, with a sentence explaining its main driver.

QUICK SELF-CHECK

Units, selected process names and production/use boundaries match; investigate a mismatch before changing assumptions.

Suggested pace: 10 minutes on the essentials, 20–40 minutes on the task, 5–10 minutes checking your result. Mandatory assignment work is additional.

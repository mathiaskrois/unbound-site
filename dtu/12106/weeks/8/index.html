01 / UNDERSTAND

What matters this week

Essential takeaways, synthesised from the module and case instructions.

01

Renewable resources can still be overused

Sustainable extraction depends on regeneration, not merely on whether a resource is labelled renewable. Fisheries illustrate how management decisions affect future availability.

02

Extraction has wider impacts

A sustainable yield does not guarantee a low biodiversity or environmental footprint. Assess the wider consequences of the extraction strategy as well as the quantity harvested.

03

Criticality is broader than physical scarcity

The criticality reading considers resource risks and vulnerability. For microfabrication, a small quantity of a hard-to-substitute material may deserve attention alongside high-volume energy or water inputs.

04

Shared resources require governance

The commons problem helps explain why individually rational extraction can undermine collective outcomes. Management rules and incentives matter to the sustainability conclusion.
